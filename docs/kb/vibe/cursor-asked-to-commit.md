# Cursor asked me to commit — what does that mean?

**Nothing scary.** Cursor is asking: "Should I save a snapshot of your code?" Say yes. Here's what's actually happening.

## The plain-English version

Your AI editor (Cursor) made changes to your code. Now it wants to **commit** them — save a snapshot with a note about what changed. This is normal. It's how code gets saved properly.

## What to do

1. **Look at what changed.** Cursor shows you a list of files. Skim it — make sure nothing looks wrong.
2. **Write a short message** describing the change, like "add login button" or "fix crash on start." (Cursor often suggests one — you can use it.)
3. **Hit commit.** Done.

## What commit does NOT do

- It does **not** upload anything. It's just a save on your computer.
- It does **not** break anything. You can always undo it: [How do I undo my last commit?](../panic/undo-last-commit.md)
- It does **not** send your code to anyone.

## Why the AI asks

Commits are save points. If the next change breaks something, you can go back to this one. The AI is being responsible — it's saving your progress before doing more.

## The one-liner

**Commit = save. That's it.** The AI wants to save before continuing. Let it.
