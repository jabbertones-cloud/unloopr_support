# What's the difference between fork and clone?

Short version: **clone = download. Fork = your own copy on GitHub.**

## Clone

`git clone` **downloads** a repository to your computer. You get the code, the history, everything — on your machine. You can work on it, but you can't push changes back unless the owner gave you permission.

## Fork

A **fork** is a copy of someone else's repository **on GitHub, under your account**. It's like photocopying a document so you can write on your copy without touching the original.

The usual flow:
1. **Fork** the repo on GitHub (click the Fork button) — now you own a copy.
2. **Clone** your fork to your computer — now you can work on it.
3. Push changes to your fork, then open a **pull request** to suggest them to the original.

## The one-liner

**Fork = "make it mine on GitHub." Clone = "download it to my computer."**

You usually do both: fork first (on GitHub), then clone your fork (to your computer).
